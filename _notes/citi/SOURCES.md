## A. The commerce side — Citi Services

**A1 · Citi's own E-Commerce page — the component list.**
<https://www.citigroup.com/global/businesses/services/treasury-and-trade-solutions/e-commerce>
Heading: *"Powering e-commerce 24/7."* The named solutions on that page, verbatim:

> Gig Economy Pay Outs · Consumer Collections · Business Collections ·
> Tokenization · Cyber Security · Reconciliation and Reporting

> "Accept consumer payments online and provide an enhanced payment experience
> with Spring by Citi."

> "Leverage virtual account capabilities and payer identification to
> automatically segregate funds."

⭐ **This is the single most useful source on the page, and it is why the
correspondence diagram works.** Citi has already named, on one page, five of the
six things a delivery order needs: take the customer's money, hold it apart, pay
the business, pay the driver, reconcile. We are not proposing a flow. We are
pointing at theirs.

**A2 · Spring by Citi.** <https://www.citibank.com/icg/sa/spring/>
> "Spring by Citi® is an ecommerce and digital payment acceptance solution,
> enabling you to accept diverse payment methods across the globe."

Launched in the U.S. in 2020 out of Treasury and Trade Solutions; live in 23
countries as of 2026.

**A3 · The continuum, and it is first-party.**
<https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/services-investor-day/payments-transcript.pdf>
Debopama Sen, Head of Payments — *2024 Services Investor Day: Payments*,
June 18, 2024:

> "...we are addressing our client's key needs... along the full payments
> continuum of accept, hold, pay and finance from facilitating payroll payments
> to gig economy payouts, like paying app developers, to managing receivables
> from their customers."

> "Our clients are looking for fully integrated solutions across the entire
> continuum of accept, hold, pay and finance as they look to scale quickly and
> globally."

⭐ **THIS SUPERSEDES THE TRADE-PRESS WARNING THIS ENTRY USED TO CARRY.** The
formulation was previously known here only through Tearsheet and American
Banker, so the page was told to paraphrase it. It is Citi's own, said by the
head of the business, on the record, with a date — so it can be quoted as
Citi's, and the page says **accept, hold, pay, finance** with no hedge and no
fourth word of ours attached. ⛔ The site briefly read "finance the gap", which
was our gloss on their word; it now reads what they said.
⭐ **And the same passage names gig economy payouts and the ride-hailing app in
the same breath as the marketplace**, which is the strongest single sentence
either party has published toward this page's argument.

**A4 · Marketplace collect-hold-payout.** Citi Services describes enabling
"marketplace and platform sellers to collect, hold and payout sellers and
merchants," and, for ride-hailing platforms, an API-backed instant payout

⚠ The collect-hold-payout phrasing is still trade press, but A3's transcript

the continuum they sit on. The courier-payout claim can stand on A1's **"Gig Economy Pay Outs"**
alone, which is Citi's own words on Citi's own page, and that is the one to use.

**A5 · Merchant finance white paper** (Citi TTS, *eCommerce: Merchant Finance*):

⛔ **NO LONGER CITED.** The 35% clause was cut from the Services column on

## B. The impact side — Citi Impact Fund

**B1 · The fund itself.**
<https://www.citigroup.com/global/our-impact/strengthening-community/citi-impact-fund>
> "The $500 million Citi Impact Fund was first launched in 2020, born out of the
> belief that venture impact investing could play a meaningful role in advancing
> economic opportunity."

Direct equity investments **from Citi's own balance sheet** into U.S.-based
companies. Four areas, quoted:

- **Social infrastructure** — "Solutions that expand access to essential
  services including housing, healthcare and transportation for underserved
  communities."
- **Financial resilience** — "Solutions that provide economically vulnerable
  Americans with opportunities to create, manage and build financial access and
  resilience."
- **Future of work** — "Solutions that create pathways to stable, quality jobs…"
- **Climate resilience**

Beyond capital, the fund offers banking solutions, networking and curated
strategic support to help companies **"reach new markets, increase demand for
their offerings or build long-term resilience."**

Totals: **58 companies and funds, $215MM+ allocated, as of June 30, 2026.**

**B2 · Meredith Shields, Head of Citi Impact Fund — 8 September 2026.**
<https://www.citigroup.com/global/news/perspectives/2026/investing-inflection-point-ai-potential-community-impact>
> "In past moments of technological leaps, such as the internet and broadband
> infrastructure, many underserved communities have been the last to benefit."

> "We can invest in solutions that help meet essential community needs while the
> technology is evolving, not after the groundwork has been laid."

⭐ **Two weeks old, by the person who runs the fund, and it is the argument for
building a neighborhood's commerce layer now rather than after.** This is the
quote the Impact section is built around.

**B3 · Housing commitment, July 2026.** $25 million toward housing access,
supply and affordability. Evidence that the fund commits to a named domain when
it sees one; use only if the page needs it.

## C. What we are NOT going to say

⛔ **Citi Social Finance is not the second door, and the draft treats it as one.**
<https://www.citigroup.com/global/our-impact/strengthening-community/social-finance>
Its language does match — market-based approaches, financial inclusion, job
creation, social infrastructure — but the unit works in **more than 40 emerging
markets**, and its stated goal is low-income households globally. The Ward is a
U.S. neighborhood. Citing it invites the first informed reader at Citi to note
that we cited the wrong desk, which costs more than the sentence earns.
▶ **The U.S. door is the Impact Fund (B1), full stop.** Social Finance may
appear in one line, correctly scoped, or not at all. Recommend not at all.

⛔ **There is no Citi-published diagram reading Buyer → eCommerce Platform →
Supplier → Delivery Driver.** It was looked for. What exists is A1: a list of
named components on Citi's own e-commerce page. The correspondence figure must
therefore be drawn as **Citi's published components against our roles**, and the
page must never claim to be reproducing a Citi drawing. ⭐ The argument survives
intact and is arguably stronger: a menu of parts we can point to by name is
harder to wave off than a picture.

⛔ **No logo, no wordmark, no arc.** Citi is set in the page's own type, like any
other proper noun. Reproducing the mark implies endorsement and is a trademark
question we have no reason to open.

⛔ **No claimed relationship, at any strength.** Not "in conversation with," not
"exploring," not "pursuing." The page's whole credibility is that it says
plainly that nothing has happened yet.

## D. The test every question in §4 has to pass

⛔ **A question whose answer we already hold is not a question, it is a
confession.** Three shapes kept getting written and all three are banned:

1. **The known yes.** *"Whether a courier can be paid as an individual"* — Citi's
   Gig Economy Pay Outs product exists to pay individual drivers. Asking it says
   we understand neither our model nor their product.
2. **The question we already answered.** Couriers are paid at the end of the
   night shift; tips are theirs entire; the courier allocation has a floor set
   per Ward; and the minimum order is sized so a courier's ticket is humane.
   ⛔ **None of that is Citi's to decide, so none of it may be asked.** What is
   theirs is whether their rails can settle that fast, and what it costs when
   the recipients are many and each amount is small.
3. **The answer on their website.** *"What payment methods are available"* is
   published. What is not published is what each one costs us at our size — and
   because processing is a pass&#8209;through here, that number reaches the
   customer.

⭐ **The reserve question came out of this pass and is the most valuable row in
the section.** A rolling reserve is the ordinary thing an acquirer does to a
young marketplace, it decides whether couriers can be paid the same night, and
nothing on either side of this page had asked about it.

### The pass over the remaining ten (2026-09-22)

Nine were rewritten and one was deleted.

⛔ **`Pricing` is gone because it was `Spring eligibility` asked a second time.**
Both rows asked whether we are too small to serve. One row asks it now, and asks
for the floor as a number.

⛔ **`Merchant of record` was asking Citi to decide something we decided.** Cary
routes the whole order and pays out the same night; The Ward is in the funds
flow by design. The row states that and asks the part that is theirs — whether
they will underwrite it.

⛔ **`KYB and KYC` contained the same known yes as the courier row** — an
individual rather than a company — and missed the question underneath it. The
Ward cannot produce a list of its residents because it never collects what that
would take, so what a neighbor must hand over to be paid, and whether Citi can
hold it instead of us, is a doctrine question wearing a compliance hat. It is
now `Courier verification`.

⭐ **A question with its consequence attached is harder to answer with a
brochure.** A chargeback that lands three weeks after a courier was paid the
same night is the actual collision between our settlement promise and their
dispute window, and that is why the refunds row now states it.

⛔ **`Business onboarding` was deleted on the next pass** (Jacob, 2026-09-22).
Its consequence sentence was doing the work the question should have done, and
what a bank asks a small business for is ordinary diligence we can find out
without spending one of these lines on it.

⭐ **`Pilot conditions` now asks for thresholds rather than encouragement,**
which is the difference between a meeting and a specification.
