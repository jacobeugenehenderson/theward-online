  <summary>What we take, and what we don’t</summary>

  <p><b>Nobody pays to be on it.</b> Revenue comes from transactions and services that happen through the platform, so it follows <b>how much a neighborhood sells</b> — not how much anyone is charged for access.</p>
  <p style="max-width:68ch;margin-top:-12px">A townie has checked in on three distinct days in a rolling fourteen, in the neighborhood, server-side; every delivery resolves to a building inside the boundary. <b>Participation is structural, not acquired.</b></p>

  <ul class="rail-list">
    <li><b><span id="pf-1">10.5%</span> of food sold</b>, after the courier is paid — and the neighborhood’s own Local Host is paid out of that before the studio keeps any of it. Processing is passed through at cost and is never marked up — what is charged is what is paid — and nothing is charged to a merchant to participate.</li>
    <li><b>New capabilities enter an existing Ward, not a new market.</b> Retail, services, tickets and events are in-boundary by construction; they do not require a separate population to be acquired.</li>
    <li><b>The unit is a bounded economy with verified-by-presence participants.</b> To a payments institution <span id="pf-2">10.5%</span> understates it: behind the flow sit merchant relationships, deposits, payments, payouts, and participation in a locally verified economy.</li>
  </ul>
