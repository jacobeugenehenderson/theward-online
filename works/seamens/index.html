Built for the Cape

The Ward gives a community a place of its own online.

It organizes a community’s buildings, businesses, institutions, events, services and people around the place they already share.

On the Outer Cape, that creates an unusual opportunity.

01Start with Provincetown

Provincetown is a compact town with a highly seasonal economy.

Its residents, businesses, institutions and visitors share the same physical place, even as the population and economy change dramatically through the year.

A Ward makes that place legible online.

02Put the local economy inside it

Cary is The Ward’s commerce layer.

It starts with neighborhood delivery: a customer buys from a local business, a local courier delivers it, and the business and courier get paid.

But delivery is only the first service. Other local purchases and services can run through Cary as well.

That means a Ward is not simply a guide to a place. It can become one of the places where local economic activity happens.

03Why Seamen’s

Seamen’s already serves the same local economy.

Its customers are the people who live here. Its business customers are the businesses a Ward would serve. Its branches, lending and banking services already operate across the communities where additional Wards could go.

The opportunity is to connect those things.

Seamen’s could help establish the first Cape Ward in Provincetown and work with us on the financial role a community bank can play inside it.

That could begin with the businesses already in the Ward and the transactions that happen through Cary. From there, we find out what else makes sense.

04Across the Cape

Truro, Wellfleet, Eastham and other Cape communities can each have a Ward of their own, built on the same underlying machinery.

For Seamen’s, that means the relationship can grow along the same geography the bank already serves.

For The Ward, it means building with an institution that already knows the businesses, the seasons and the communities involved.
