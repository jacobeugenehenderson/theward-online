Net, against neighborhoods

What crosses the rails

The platform is free. Nobody pays to be on it. We take a cut of what is bought through it, so revenue follows how much a neighborhood sells — not how much anyone is charged for access.

A townie has checked in on three distinct days in a rolling fourteen, in the neighborhood, server-side; every delivery resolves to a building inside the boundary. Participation is structural, not acquired.

Delivery only

—
where we are

One module, in the neighborhoods we run.

  • 6.3% of food sold, after the courier and the neighborhood are paid
  • The Local Host runs the place — Places, local administrators, businesses, and moderation (§3.2, §3.3). Its share is what that is paid with
  • Processing is pass-through at cost. Never revenue

Many wards

—
—

The same rails under every neighborhood poured.

  • Each pour is paid for once — commissioned, never sold to the neighborhood
  • The unit is a bounded economy with verified-by-presence participants — a place, not a market
  • ⛔ To a payments institution 6.3% understates it: behind the flow sit the merchant relationships, the deposits, and a channel to residents verified by presence
  • ⛔ It survives only while the platform is free. Monetise the relationship directly and there is nothing left but a map

The service charge and the commission are sourced; the Local Host’s 4.2% is a proposal, not yet set, and every volume figure above is invented. The entity split is open — org-structure.md says decide it before place-order ships.

What is true today

Built and standing

  • ~136,000 lines across eight surfaces, six app entrypoints
  • Lafayette Square fully installed — 1,729 buildings with facades, years, architects, historic status
  • A second under way at Hi-Pointe / DeMun
  • Live at lafayette-square.com; handbook and seven role guides at theward.online
  • Couriers, dispatch, live map, phone verification — running
  • Commerce built: item identity, price of record, 86, the guardian write path, the order contract

Not yet true

  • No revenue has ever come in
  • The commerce layer is written but has never run — four deploy steps outstanding
  • Two lawyer items open: the sales-tax ruling, the alcohol permit
  • Insurance unpriced, and a condition of switching delivery on
  • The entity split — infrastructure vs. operator — undecided
  • No fiscal sponsor chosen; no board recruited
  • Identity and guardianship live in a spreadsheet. It works, and it is not where they should stay

What the roster means

  • A studio at full strength is a seven-figure annual budget — a different class of funder than a first community grant
  • Which is why absorption is not the fallback path: an established organisation already employs these five engineers
  • Custodial staffing keeps it alive and pours towns. It does not develop the product
  • Assembling this team from grants alone is a multi-year fundraising project before a line is written
  • Software got cheaper to write. The pour, and the specialisms above, did not
