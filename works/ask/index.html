What we take, and what we don’t

Nobody pays to be on it. Revenue comes from transactions and services that happen through the platform, so it follows how much a neighborhood sells — not how much anyone is charged for access.

A townie has checked in on three distinct days in a rolling fourteen, in the neighborhood, server-side; every delivery resolves to a building inside the boundary. Participation is structural, not acquired.

  • 10.5% of food sold, after the courier is paid — and the neighborhood’s own Local Host is paid out of that before the studio keeps any of it. Processing is passed through at cost and is never marked up — what is charged is what is paid — and nothing is charged to a merchant to participate.
  • New capabilities enter an existing Ward, not a new market. Retail, services, tickets and events are in-boundary by construction; they do not require a separate population to be acquired.
  • The unit is a bounded economy with verified-by-presence participants. To a payments institution 10.5% understates it: behind the flow sit merchant relationships, deposits, payments, payouts, and participation in a locally verified economy.

What is true today

Built and standing

  • ~136,000 lines across eight surfaces, six app entrypoints
  • Lafayette Square fully installed — 1,729 buildings with facades, years, architects, historic status
  • A second under way at Hi-Pointe / DeMun
  • Live at lafayette-square.com; handbook and seven role guides at theward.online
  • Couriers, dispatch, live map, phone verification — running
  • Commerce built: item identity, price of record, 86, the guardian write path, the order contract

Not yet true

  • Pre-revenue — commercial operation has not begun
  • The commerce layer is written but has never run — four deploy steps outstanding
  • Two legal items remain open
  • Insurance unpriced, and a condition of switching delivery on
  • The entity split — infrastructure vs. operator — undecided
  • No fiscal sponsor chosen; no board recruited
  • Identity and guardianship live in a spreadsheet. It works, and it is not where they should stay

What the roster means

  • A studio at full strength is a seven-figure annual budget — a different class of funder than a first community grant
  • Absorption changes the staffing equation: an established organisation may already employ these five disciplines.
  • Custodial staffing keeps it alive and pours towns. It does not develop the product
  • Assembling this team from grants alone is a multi-year fundraising project before a line is written
  • The existing Platform reduces what has to be built. It does not eliminate the people required to operate and extend it.
