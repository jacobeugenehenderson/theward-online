Built Toward Citi

The Ward is independent infrastructure for real neighborhoods. As its commerce layer is built, it is being built to fit what Citi has already published.

01The CorrespondenceTheir components, our roles

The reason is practical rather than aspirational. A neighborhood delivery order needs someone to take the customer’s payment, keep the funds separate, pay the business, pay the courier, and reconcile all of it. Citi’s e‑commerce business names every one of those on its own pages: consumer collections, acceptance through Spring by Citi, virtual accounts that “automatically segregate funds,” domestic ACH payments, gig economy pay outs, and reconciliation and reporting.

Those capabilities correspond closely to what Cary needs.

What The Ward supplies is the part a processor does not: the place, the neighborhood’s own network of businesses and couriers, and the operating system that decides what an order means before any money moves.

02The BuildWhat gets built, in what order

This is the current development path. Each step produces something that can be tested before moving to the next.

Now

Build compatibleKeep The Ward’s commerce authority independent of whoever moves the money. The system decides what an order is, who is owed and what the rules are; the processor executes. Document what Spring requires. Design the payment layer so a processor sits underneath it rather than inside it. Settle the merchant‑of‑record and sub‑merchant structure. Write down what courier payout actually demands.

Next

Prove one WardReal businesses, real orders, real couriers, in one neighborhood. Record transaction volume, merchant retention, courier economics, fulfillment and repeat use — and the failures: refunds, chargebacks, disputes, and what it actually costs to run.

Then

Prove it travelsNeighborhoods beyond the first are already underway, with different geography, institutions and local economies. Run the same commerce layer in a second Ward and document what changes — and what does not.

Then

A defined pilotAt that point there is something concrete to test with Citi: a working operating system, transaction history, a known set of integration requirements, and a bounded deployment for Spring.

Later

An institutional hostA Citi‑backed deployment becomes one available host model: financial infrastructure and institutional scale from one side, the neighborhood layer from the other.

03The Two DoorsWhy this institution and not another

There are two distinct points of connection with Citi: its payments business and its community investment work.

Citi Services / Commerce

Citi’s e‑commerce business is organised around a continuum: accept the payment, hold the funds, pay everyone owed, finance. Its own pages list consumer collections, acceptance through Spring by Citi, virtual accounts that segregate funds, domestic ACH payments, gig economy pay outs and reconciliation.

A neighborhood delivery order is a small instance of exactly that shape — a two‑sided marketplace where the seller is not the operator.

Citi Impact / Community Infrastructure

The Citi Impact Fund invests Citi’s own balance sheet in U.S. companies working on affordability, access and resilience, across four areas that include financial resilience and social infrastructure — “solutions that expand access to essential services including housing, healthcare and transportation for underserved communities.”

Beyond capital it says it helps companies “reach new markets, increase demand for their offerings or build long‑term resilience.” As of June 30, 2026: 58 companies and funds, more than $215 million allocated.

The commercial and community cases are different. The Ward gives them common ground.

04What We Need

What we need from Citi is answers

Every line below is a question whose answer changes what we build. We would rather design against a real constraint now than discover it during a pilot.

Merchant of recordCary routes the whole order and pays the business and the courier the same night, so The Ward is in the funds flow by design. Whether Citi will underwrite that as one merchant with businesses beneath it, or require every business to stand as its own.
Spring eligibilitySpring is sold to institutional clients. Whether a platform this small is servable at all, where the floor sits, and what it costs down there.
Courier verificationThe Ward cannot produce a list of its residents because it never collects what that would take. What a neighbor must hand over before their first payout, and whether Citi can hold it so that we never do.
SettlementWhen funds become available to pay out from, and whether a marketplace at our size carries a rolling reserve or a hold before they do.
Courier payoutsWe pay couriers at the end of the night. Which rails settle that fast, what each costs, and whether that cost is charged per payout or per batch — a Ward pays many small recipients every night, and that shape decides the economics.
TipsWhether a tip can be carried as the courier’s money from the start, so it never lands in our revenue and never has to be handed back out of it.
RefundsWhere dispute liability sits when one party cooked it and another carried it — and what happens to a courier who was already paid at the end of that night when the chargeback lands three weeks later.
Payment methodsWhether pay‑by‑bank and instant debit are open to us, and what each method costs against a card. Processing is a pass‑through here, so the method a customer picks changes what that customer is charged.
ReconciliationWhether our own order identifier survives into Citi’s reporting, and whether a payout can carry the split that produced it, so the two ledgers agree without a person in the middle.
POS coexistenceWhether a business can keep its own acquirer at the counter while The Ward is merchant of record online, and what that leaves it to reconcile.
Data boundariesWhat Citi retains about a neighbor who buys a sandwich, how long, and whether any of it may be used for anything other than settling that sandwich.
Pilot conditionsWhat has to be true — volume, entity, capital, insurance, controls — before Citi would put Spring under a live Ward, stated as thresholds rather than as encouragement.

Built to remain independent

Spring is a target integration, not a dependency. The payment layer remains separable from The Ward itself, so the work above improves the platform regardless of who ultimately provides the financial infrastructure.
